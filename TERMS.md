# Data terms and privacy

RavensPort runs under conditions set by the IEEE SaTML 2027 programme chairs, which the organisers have accepted. The conditions are the following notice.

> **Ravensport Competition: Data Terms and Privacy Notice**
> *SaTML 2027 Competition*
>
> **Competition submissions.** A submission is the team's technical solution to the competition (including source code, technical description and methodology). Participants retain ownership of their submissions. Organiser-held submissions will be used only for scoring and will be accessible only to the Alan Turing Institute researchers managing the competition. The UK National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) will have no access to non-public submissions or findings. Submissions will be deleted within one month after the competition and will not be used for any other purpose, including commercial or governmental use.
>
> **Participant information.** This comprises participants’ names, email addresses and organisations, plus the submission’s public source-code link. Participants’ names, organisations and public source-code links will be published with the competition results and benchmark. Email addresses will be used only to administer the competition and will not be published. Participant information will be processed only to manage the competition. Any participant information not published with the results will be deleted within one month after the competition and will not be shared outside the Institute. The full Turing Privacy Notice is available [here](https://www.turing.ac.uk/turing-policy-statement-legal/privacy-policy).
>
> **Public release and licences.** After the competition, the winners, runners-up and aggregate scores will be made public. The complete Ravensport benchmark, including withheld attacks, will be released under the MIT License. Winning teams will publish their own code under a suitable permissive open-source licence, such as the MIT License; the organisers will not retain or distribute it. Any third-party or previously released component retains its existing licence, which the MIT License does not replace. The restrictions above apply only to non-public material: anyone, including the organisers and NCSC, may use publicly released material under its applicable licence.
